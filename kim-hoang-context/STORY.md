# Tranh Kim Hoàng

A game-like drawing app for children about 5 to 8 who have limited fine motor control. It copies the order of a Kim Hoàng print so the child can do the work a village visit would ask for, without needing a steady brush or a real woodblock.

Kim Hoàng painting is an 18th-century woodcut form from Kim Hoàng village, Hoài Đức, Hanoi. Paper is dyed red first. A carved block stamps black ink. The artist then colors the sheet by hand. A flood in 1915 destroyed most of the original blocks. The village stopped making the prints by 1945. The app uses two surviving motif types: the pig (solid black stamp, reserved white rubbed in later) and the rooster (outline stamp, then seven colour plates, then a poem).

The child picks a block, carves the waste wood by tapping or dragging, rolls black ink, chooses chu sa or son đỏ paper, presses the block, colors the print, and (for the rooster) places the scanned poem. Home also opens a gallery of village prints by Linh Pham (including the Bộ Kiều mini album). That gallery is not a step in the woodblock game. There is no timer. Wrong plates buzz and do nothing. Undo sits on the color step. Labels are Vietnamese first, English under them.

The project is a high school portfolio piece: product design, cultural research, and a working Expo app on a physical iPhone or iPad. There is no account system and no App Store build required for the demo.
